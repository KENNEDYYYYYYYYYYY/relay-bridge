Built for people who bridge on purpose

No dashboards of noise, no mystery fees. Relay Bridge keeps the four things that matter in plain sight.

A real quote, not an estimate

Every quote comes straight from Relay’s API for your exact route — output amount, fee and steps — before your wallet is even asked to sign.

The fee, up front

On the verified quote, bridging 1 USDC costs 0.022335 USDC and lands 0.977665 USDC on Arbitrum. That breakdown is on screen before step one — never buried after the fact.

Tracked until it lands

The app polls Relay’s status endpoint after you deposit and follows the fill through waiting and pending to success — or refund — instead of leaving you to refresh an explorer.

A receipt you can point to

Each completed bridge is saved with its amounts and links to both explorers — the deposit on Base and the fill on Arbitrum — so any run can be verified on-chain in one click.

How it works

Four steps, start to receipt. The app handles the Relay calls; your wallet handles the signatures.

01

Connect your wallet

MetaMask, Coinbase Wallet or Rabby — any wallet holding USDC and a little ETH on Base. No account, no sign-up.

02

Get the live quote

The app asks Relay for 1 USDC, Base → Arbitrum, and shows the output, the 0.022335 USDC fee and the exact steps before anything moves.

03

Approve, then deposit

Two wallet signatures: approve USDC, then make the deposit. Nothing executes without both, and you can stop after either one.

04

Track it to success

Status updates live until Relay confirms the fill on Arbitrum. The receipt — amounts plus both explorer links — is saved to your history.

Your keys stay yours. So does the API key.

Relay Bridge is non-custodial by construction: it never holds your funds or your keys. Funds move wallet → Relay → your wallet on Arbitrum, and every movement is a transaction you personally signed.

The same discipline applies on our side of the integration. The Relay API key lives on the server only — it is never shipped to the browser, never in the page code, and the app only ever displays its last four characters.

See bridge preview
  • You sign everything. Approve and deposit are separate signatures in your own wallet — the app cannot move funds on its own.
  • Key stays server-side. The browser talks to the app’s own quote endpoint; the Relay API key is added on the server and never exposed to the frontend.
  • Status from the source. Fill status is polled from Relay until it resolves to success or refund — no guessed states, no silent failures.
  • Proof on-chain. Every receipt links to the deposit on Basescan and the fill on Arbiscan, so the result never has to be taken on trust.
